# Synthesis Report: Multi-Omic Pathway Analysis in Mus musculus

## Key Findings

- **A coordinated, transient MHC class I antigen-presentation response peaks at 12h** across multiple viral-infection pathways (HSV-1, HTLV-1, HPV), with H2-K1, H2-Q1, H2-Q2, H2-Bl, H2-Q4, and H2-D1 all showing identical transient-peak profiles (+2.65 at 12h), suggesting a shared regulatory module rather than independent gene regulation [1,4,6,13]. - **Sustained anti-apoptotic/pro-survival reprogramming dominates the late phase (18–24h)**, driven by monotonic Bcl2 upregulation (1.51→3.06) across apoptosis, autophagy, and AGE-RAGE pathways, alongside flat/declining pro-apoptotic Bcl2l11 (BIM), indicating a net shift toward cell survival [2,5,9]. - **Chromatin accessibility (DNase-seq) changes precede transcriptional output**, with early (0h) DNase peaks at loci encoding proteasome subunits, Smad3, Jak2, and Cdc42 preceding later transcriptional changes, suggesting chromatin priming as an initiating event [7,9,10,15]. - **miRNA-mediated regulation is a dominant post-transcriptional layer**, particularly in efferocytosis (p=0.0059) and autophagy (p=0.0165) pathways, with progressive miRNA depletion releasing targets like Crkl, Dusp16, and Itpr1 [7,14]. - **Cell-cycle repression (Ccnd2 downregulation) with concordant miRNA increases** appears across HPV, HTLV-1, and cellular senescence pathways, indicating miRNA-mediated dampening of proliferation as a shared feature [4,6,13]. ---

## Detailed Pathway Analysis

### A. The sustained Bcl2 rise (1.51→3.06) indicates an anti-apoptotic survival program countering virus-induced cell death. Staggered SR splicing factor peaks (Srsf3 at 12h, Srsf5 at 18h) suggest sequential alternative-splicing reprogramming. The transient 12h dip in Mavs (−3.33) and Syk proteomics (−4.28) may reflect a window of viral immune evasion. Nuclear transport disruption (Ranbp3, Xpo1) may affect viral or host transcription factor localization. The sustained Ccnd2 decline with rising miRNAs points to miRNA-mediated dampening of cell-cycle entry. The transient Rad50 dip (−4.57 at 12h) suggests an acute DNA-repair/checkpoint perturbation, consistent with the DNA-damage response link to senescence [8,9]. ### B. The transient Parp1 wave (dip to −1.60 at 0h, rise to 1.30 at 12h) suggests an early acute stress/DNA-damage response that resolves as survival signaling takes over. The highly significant TF layer (p=0.0025) implies active upstream regulation. Dynll1 shows the strongest signal (monotonic-up to 5.28 at 24h), with early DNase accessibility peaks (0h) preceding expression changes. The transient Bax dip-rebound (−1.29 at 12h, +0.88 at 18h) indicates acute, reversible signaling rather than stable commitment. The weak expression-layer significance (p=0.0863) suggests regulation is primarily at chromatin/TF levels with mRNA changes still emerging. The sustained Bcl2 rise and late Lamp1 decline (−1.78 at 24h) imply a shift away from lysosomal/autophagic flux toward survival signaling. The transient Rab7 pulse (1.37 at 12h, −1.75 at 18h) suggests a brief window of autophagosome maturation that is not sustained. ### C. Sustained Bcl2 upregulation with simultaneous Smad3/Jak2/Mapk1 repression indicates a shift toward pro-survival signaling and away from TGF-β-driven transcription. The opposing Bcl2 (up) versus Smad3/Jak2 (down) trajectories may reflect negative feedback within the AGE-RAGE cascade. The striking Pard3 biphasic profile (trough −6.8 at 12h, rebound +3.47 at 18h) indicates transient apical polarity disruption. Progressive Smad3 (−3.7) and Btrc (−0.55) repression, alongside proteomic loss of 14-3-3 proteins (Ywhaq −1.86, Ywhab −1.72 at 12h), implies reduced negative regulation of the Hippo cascade. Sustained Fzd7 upregulation (+1.07 at 18h) indicates activation of upstream receptor inputs. Early PLC/Ca²⁺ signaling (Plcb3 high at baseline, Cacna1i peak at 12h) precedes sustained Map2k1 induction (1.28 at 24h) and transient Mapk1 activation (1.09 at 12h, −1.63 at 18h). Progressive Pik3r1 decline (−1.03 at 18h) indicates PI3K/Akt arm repression, rebalancing toward the MAPK branch. ### D. The transient St13 induction (+2.18 at 12h) fits canonical heat-shock co-chaperone response, while opposing Ptges3 profile (−1.92 at 18h) indicates differential co-chaperone regulation. Flat mRNA with late proteomic peaks for several genes implies substantial post-transcriptional regulation. The opposing late Pycr2 rise (+1.24 at 18h) suggests compensatory rebalancing. Transcript-protein decoupling (Srm mRNA down, protein peak at 12h) indicates post-transcriptional regulation. Late miRNA repression of Itpr1 (−1.92, −1.57 at 18h) is mechanistically coherent with SCA calcium-signaling pathophysiology, though not translated to transcript/protein changes within the window. Transient engulfment machinery activation (Cd36 peak 0.86 at 18h, Elmo1 peak 2.93 at 18h) with opposing Mapk1 dip at 18h suggests a controlled, self-limiting cascade with Dusp4-mediated negative feedback. ### E. The late, sustained Dynll1 induction with flat early phase argues against acute degranulation, instead suggesting delayed transcriptional reprogramming of granule-trafficking machinery. Discordant mRNA/protein profiles for several genes indicate substantial post-transcriptional regulation. ---

## Suggested Follow-Up Experiments

### 1. **ChIP-seq for H3K27ac and H3K4me1 at MHC Class I Locus (Priority: High)**
- **Rationale**: The identical transient-peak profiles of six H2 genes at 12h strongly suggest a shared enhancer/promoter regulatory event. Understanding the chromatin state at this locus will reveal whether this is a single coordinated regulatory module or independent gene activation. - **Technique**: ChIP-seq for active enhancer (H3K27ac, H3K4me1) and promoter (H3K4me3) marks at 0, 6, 12, 18, 24h timepoints, with ATAC-seq for accessibility confirmation. - **Expected Outcome**: Identification of a shared enhancer element or super-enhancer driving coordinated MHC-I activation, potentially revealing the upstream transcription factor (e.g., IRF1, NF-κB) responsible. ### 2. **Bcl2/Bcl2l11 Functional Perturbation with Live-Cell Apoptosis Imaging (Priority: High)**
- **Rationale**: Bcl2 shows the most reproducible signal across pathways (apoptosis, autophagy, AGE-RAGE), but the functional consequence of its sustained upregulation is unknown. The flat Bcl2l11 (BIM) with declining miRNAs suggests post-transcriptional regulation. - **Technique**: CRISPR interference (CRISPRi) of Bcl2 and/or Bcl2l11 in the relevant cell model, with real-time apoptosis imaging (e.g., Annexin V-GFP, caspase-3 FRET reporter) over 24h. - **Expected Outcome**: Demonstration that Bcl2 upregulation is functionally required for the observed pro-survival shift, and that Bcl2l11 miRNA-mediated repression contributes to BIM silencing. ### 3. **miRNA Pulldown and Target Validation for Itpr1 and Crkl (Priority: Medium-High)**
- **Rationale**: miRNA-seq is the dominant or contributing layer in efferocytosis, autophagy, and SCA pathways, but the discordance between miRNA changes and flat mRNA profiles suggests either delayed effects or miRNA-mediated translational repression. - **Technique**: Ago2-CLIP (crosslinking and immunoprecipitation) to identify miRNA-mRNA interactions at 12h and 18h, followed by luciferase reporter validation for prioritized targets (Itpr1, Crkl, Dusp16, Ccnd2). - **Expected Outcome**: Direct evidence of miRNA-mediated regulation of these targets, with identification of the specific miRNAs responsible and their mechanism (mRNA degradation vs. translational repression). ### 4. **Time-Resolved Nuclear-Cytoplasmic Fractionation with Proteomics (Priority: Medium)**
- **Rationale**: Nuclear transport disruption appears in both HTLV-1 (Ranbp3, Xpo1) and heat stress (Nup85, Nup50, Nup133) pathways, suggesting altered nucleocytoplasmic shuttling as a shared stress mechanism. - **Technique**: Nuclear-cytoplasmic fractionation at 0, 6, 12, 18, 24h followed by TMT-labeled quantitative proteomics to identify proteins with altered subcellular localization. - **Expected Outcome**: Identification of transcription factors and signaling proteins whose nuclear-cytoplasmic distribution changes over time, revealing the functional consequences of NPC component repression. ### 5. **Chromatin Priming Validation with Inhibitor Time-Course (Priority: Medium)**
- **Rationale**: Multiple pathways show early DNase accessibility peaks (0h) preceding later transcriptional changes, suggesting chromatin priming. The causal relationship between these events is untested. - **Technique**: Treatment with chromatin remodeler inhibitors (e.g., BRD4 inhibitor JQ1, HDAC inhibitor SAHA) at 0h, followed by RNA-seq at 12h and 24h to assess whether early chromatin remodeling is required for late transcriptional responses. - **Expected Outcome**: Demonstration that early chromatin accessibility is functionally required for the observed late transcriptional changes, identifying the specific remodelers involved. ---

## Limitations and Caveats

### Data Quality Issues
- **Single-omic dominance**: Most pathways are driven by one or two omic layers, with others non-significant. This limits cross-omic validation. These discordances complicate mechanistic interpretation. - **Identical H2-family values**: The identical profiles across six MHC class I genes likely reflect a single shared measurement or locus-level event rather than independent regulation [1,4,6,13]. ### Missing Evidence
- **No pathway-specific literature retrieved**: All batch reports note the absence of pathway-specific literature, making interpretations data-driven and hypothesis-generating rather than literature-anchored. - **Limited time resolution**: The 24h window with timepoints at 0, 2, 6, 12, 18, 24h may miss both earlier acute responses and later sustained changes. The 12h peak in MHC-I and the 18h dips in several genes could represent sampling artifacts. - **No protein-level confirmation for most pathways**: Proteomics layers are non-significant for the majority of pathways, and where present, show noisy, discordant profiles. ### Interpretive Caveats
- **Transient vs. sustained**: Many changes are transient (MHC-I peak at 12h, Pard3 trough at 12h, Rab7 pulse at 12h), making conclusions about sustained pathway engagement limited. - **Post-transcriptional regulation uncertainty**: Where miRNA and DNase layers are significant but mRNA is flat, the functional consequences are unclear — regulation may be translational, may require longer timescales, or may represent non-productive regulatory noise.
